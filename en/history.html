History of the church

History of the Church of the Nativity of Christ

From a family place of worship on the old Bukiškis estate to a living Orthodox parish: a story of people, loss and careful restoration.

01

A place of worship in the history of Bukiškis

The small Orthodox Church of the Nativity of Christ stands in Bukiškis, near Vilnius. Its history brings together the story of an old estate, the memory of the family who founded the church and the life of today’s parish. Over the course of its history, the church has witnessed changes in the ownership of the surrounding estate, war, many years of use for storage and its eventual restoration.

Today, people come here for worship, to have their children baptised, to be married, to pray for loved ones and to remember the departed. Behind the familiar appearance of a functioning church lies the work of several generations. To understand what this place means, we need to remember both those who built it and those who, decades later, helped restore its original purpose.

02

The estate and the founding family

The church’s history is closely connected with Aleksandr Mikhailovich Bozheryanov and his wife, Praskovya Nikolayevna. According to N. Ya. Ivanova’s archival research, it was Praskovya Nikolayevna who purchased the Bukiškis estate in 1866. This date marks the estate’s acquisition by the family; it should not be treated as the established date of the church’s construction.

In the second half of the nineteenth century, a church was built on the estate at Aleksandr Mikhailovich’s initiative. It was intended for family prayer and became a place where its founders were remembered. The church was originally consecrated in honour of the Protection of the Most Holy Mother of God.

The church formed part of the estate as a whole. Ivanova’s study describes the manor house, outbuildings, park and lakes. Both the settlement and the surrounding landscape gradually changed, yet the church retained its connection with the early history of this place. Details about the estate’s owners and their descendants can be found in N. Ya. Ivanova’s separate study.

Sources for this section: [1][3]

03

The Church of the Protection: prayer and remembrance

The church was built in the Neo-Byzantine style. Its compact form, arched openings and dome gave it a distinctive presence within the estate. Its dedication to the Protection of the Most Holy Mother of God expressed the Orthodox tradition of prayer for her intercession and help.

Originally, it was a private family church. It housed the Bozheryanov family burial vault, bringing together the prayers of the living and remembrance of family members who had died. The church therefore served both as a place of worship and as a memorial to the family.

What we know about the estate and its family burials comes from documents, historical research and the recollections of local residents. These accounts help us see the human lives behind the architecture. Where precise dates or circumstances remain uncertain, the memory of the place itself calls for care and attention.

Sources for this section: [1]

04

War and years of neglect

The estate’s former way of life changed during the twentieth century. Its buildings were put to new uses, and the church faced particularly difficult times. According to Vilnius District Municipality, it was badly damaged during the Second World War.

For many years afterwards, it served as a storehouse for an educational institution. The historical study describes the desecration of the family burial vault and the installation of a generator in the basement. Vibration, damp and a lack of proper maintenance worsened the condition of the building. It fell into disrepair and was not used for worship for many years.

Surviving photographs show damaged walls, roofing and vaults exposed to the weather. They help convey the scale of the restoration that followed. This period is remembered above all as a time when a sacred place was lost to worship, and as a reminder of the responsibility involved in caring for cultural and spiritual heritage.

Sources for this section: [3][1]

05

Return and restoration

In the late 1990s, the building was returned to the Orthodox Church. A new chapter began: what remained had to be preserved, and the church had to be made ready for worship again. Parish historical accounts date the beginning of the restoration work to 2002.

Metropolitan Chrysostom (Martishkin) holds a special place in the church’s revival. His care and sustained involvement helped make the restoration possible. The work drew on surviving information about the building’s appearance and restoration drawings. It required time, resources and the efforts of people who cared about the future of this small church.

Restoring the building and bringing back regular worship were successive stages of the same work. Beyond the construction itself lay a wider purpose: to make the church once again a place where a community could gather, the Sacraments could be celebrated and those whose lives were connected with Bukiškis could be remembered in prayer.

Sources for this section: [1][2]

06

2011: the beginning of a new parish life

Worship resumed in the restored church in the summer of 2011. According to the parish’s account, services began with the blessing of Archbishop Innocent. Fr Maksim Krutolevich has served here since that time.

The restored church is dedicated to the Nativity of Christ. Its original dedication to the Protection of the Mother of God remains part of its history. Its present name turns the parish towards the Gospel account of the Saviour’s birth, while the return of church life marked a new beginning for this place.

The everyday life of the parish gradually grew around worship. Sunday Liturgy, major church feasts, baptisms, weddings and prayers for the living and the departed once again gave the restored building its central purpose. Families came to know the church as a familiar place of prayer and spiritual support.

Sources for this section: [2]

07

The continuing care of the church

After worship resumed, caring for the building and grounds became an ongoing shared task for the parish and its benefactors. In 2021, the cladding on the steps in front of the entrance was replaced. In 2023, damaged brickwork on the turrets on the south and north sides of the church was restored. In 2024, the sanctuary walls were repainted.

In 2026, the roof was repainted, as were the interior walls and dome, the sanctuary, the narthex and the entrance door. This work helps preserve the building and keep it suitable for worship. Each undertaking reflects the time, resources and voluntary work of individual people.

Work also continues to improve the rooms used for parish gatherings. The church needs regular care, so its history encompasses both major restoration projects and everyday attention: noticing damage in time, tending the grounds and helping prepare for a feast day.

Sources for this section: [4]

08

The living history of the parish

Today, the Church of the Nativity of Christ in Bukiškis brings together parishioners and visitors from the surrounding area and Vilnius. Services, Sacraments and other rites are celebrated here, and pastoral care is offered. The current service schedule helps people plan their visit and prepare for worship.

The parish preserves historical photographs and records, and shares information about faith and church life through its website and social media. This is another way of caring for its memory: making the church’s history accessible both to those already connected with it and to those discovering it for the first time.

The story of the Bukiškis church includes creation, loss and restoration. Preserving that story means remembering its founders, giving thanks for those who helped bring it back to life and caring for the people who come here today. Its history continues in the prayer, fellowship and good works of the parish.

Sources for this section: [2][4]
